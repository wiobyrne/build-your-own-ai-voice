# Examples

This file shows what a finished setup can look like once you have filled in the template.

It is not meant to be copied word-for-word. It is meant to give you a shape to aim for.

---

## Example voice setup

**Name:** Alex Rivera

**What Alex writes about:** digital literacy, classroom technology, community learning, and practical reflections on using AI well

**Audience:** teachers, librarians, community educators, and curious non-specialists who want plain-language explanations without hype

**Voice in one sentence:** Alex writes in connected paragraphs that explain ideas clearly, use concrete examples, and move from a specific story to a larger point without sounding preachy.

**Specific patterns:**

- Opens with a concrete situation or question, not a slogan
- Explains technical terms the first time they appear
- Uses real-world examples instead of abstract buzzwords
- Writes in paragraphs, not stacked fragments
- Uses short sentences for emphasis, but not as a repeated rhythm
- Avoids corporate jargon and motivational poster language

**Negative example:**

> In today’s rapidly evolving landscape, educators must leverage innovative solutions to optimize outcomes. This groundbreaking platform empowers stakeholders to unlock transformational potential through seamless integration and scalable excellence.

## Example sample selection

If Alex were filling out `template.modelfile`, the samples might look like this:

- **Sample 1: explanatory**
  - A paragraph from a blog post explaining what digital literacy means in plain language
- **Sample 2: reflective**
  - A section from a newsletter about trying a new tool and noticing where it helped and where it did not
- **Sample 3: argumentative**
  - A paragraph from an essay arguing that schools should teach students how AI tools shape writing
- **Sample 4: practical**
  - A passage that walks through a simple process for choosing a tool or evaluating a draft

The key is not to be perfect. The key is to pick passages that sound like the same person across different kinds of writing.

---

## Example test result

After building the model, Alex might test it with a prompt like:

> Write a short opening paragraph about why teachers should be careful with AI-generated lesson plans.

A good result would:

- sound calm rather than alarmist
- explain the tradeoff without talking down to anyone
- use ordinary words
- feel like a real person thinking through the problem

If the result sounds too polished, too corporate, or too generic, Alex should go back and improve the sample passages before changing the rules again.
